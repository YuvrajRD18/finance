Demo 9-1: Inventory Ownership (9 Scenarios)

Finn Corporation ending inventory physical count = $600,000 at 12/31. Evaluate 9 scenarios for correct ownership at year-end.

Key Concepts:
• FOB Shipping Point – Title transfers to buyer when goods are shipped.
• FOB Destination – Title transfers to buyer when goods arrive at destination.
• Consignment Goods – Belong to consignor (owner), not consignee (holder).
• Inventory – Only goods held for resale, NOT office supplies or other assets.

Scenario 1

Finn shipped $55,000 to Hemlock FOB Shipping Point on Dec 28. Not in count.

NO $0 adjustment
FOB shipping point = title passed to Hemlock on Dec 28. Finn doesn't own it. Properly excluded.

Scenario 2

$95,000 goods shipped TO Finn FOB Destination on Dec 27, still in transit. Not in count.

NO $0 adjustment
FOB destination = title passes on arrival. Still in transit = seller owns. Properly excluded.

Scenario 3

Finn received $25,000 on Jan 2. Shipped FOB Shipping Point on Dec 26 by Yanice. Not in count.

YES +$25,000 adjustment
FOB shipping point = Finn owned since Dec 26 (shipping date). Improperly excluded. Add it back.

Scenario 4

Finn shipped $51,000 to Ehler FOB Destination on Dec 30. Arrived Jan 8. Not in count.

YES +$51,000 adjustment
FOB destination = Finn owns until arrival. Goods still in transit at 12/31. Improperly excluded. Add it back.

Scenario 5

$42,000 received Jan 2, shipped FOB Destination on Dec 29. Added to count in anticipation of arrival.

NO −$42,000 adjustment
FOB destination = title passes on delivery (Jan 2), NOT Dec 31. Improperly included. Remove it.

Scenario 6

$38,000 goods Finn holds on consignment for Nader Corporation. Included in count.

NO −$38,000 adjustment
Consignment goods belong to consignor (Nader), not consignee (Finn). Improperly included. Remove it.

Scenario 7

$17,000 office supplies in Finn's warehouse for staff use. Included in count.

NO −$17,000 adjustment
Office supplies ≠ merchandise inventory. They're a separate asset. Improperly included. Remove it.

Scenario 8

$87,000 of Finn's goods held on consignment BY Barrett Corp. Not in count.

YES +$87,000 adjustment
Finn is consignor and still owns goods even though Barrett holds them. Improperly excluded. Add it back.

Scenario 9

Order completed Dec 29 (cost $29,000, sell $40,000), boxed on dock. Shipper picked up Jan 1. FOB Shipping Point. Not in count.

YES +$29,000 (at cost) adjustment
FOB shipping point = title passes at pickup (Jan 1). Finn still owns at 12/31. Improperly excluded. Add it back at cost, not selling price.

Summary Calculation

Adjustments: +$25,000 + $51,000 − $42,000 − $38,000 − $17,000 + $87,000 + $29,000 = +$95,000

Finn's Correct 12/31 Inventory Balance: $600,000 + $95,000 = $695,000
Demo 9-2: Calculating COGS & Ending Inventory Using 3 Methods

Ohm Electronics Data:

ItemUnitsCost/UnitTotal
Beginning Inventory3,000$5$15,000
Purchase 17,000$7$49,000
Goods Available for Sale10,000$64,000

Units Sold: 8,400  |  Ending Inventory: 1,600 units

FIFO First-In, First-Out

Ending Inventory: 1,600 units × $7 = $11,200
COGS: $64,000 − $11,200 = $52,800
Proof (sold oldest first): 3,000 × $5 = $15,000 + 5,400 × $7 = $37,800 → $52,800

LIFO Last-In, First-Out

Ending Inventory: 1,600 units × $5 = $8,000
COGS: $64,000 − $8,000 = $56,000
Proof (sold newest first): 7,000 × $7 = $49,000 + 1,400 × $5 = $7,000 → $56,000

AVG Average-Cost (Weighted Average)

Weighted Average Cost = $64,000 ÷ 10,000 = $6.40 per unit
Ending Inventory: 1,600 × $6.40 = $10,240
COGS: 8,400 × $6.40 = $53,760
Proof: $64,000 − $10,240 = $53,760

Summary Comparison

MetricFIFOLIFOAvg Cost
Goods Available for Sale$64,000$64,000$64,000
COGS$52,800$56,000$53,760
Ending Inventory$11,200$8,000$10,240
Key Insight: In a period of RISING PRICES, average-cost values fall BETWEEN FIFO and LIFO for both Ending Inventory and COGS.
Demo 9-3: Calculating Ending Inventory Using 3 Methods

McLanie Company (First Month of Operations):

ItemUnitsCost/UnitTotal
Beginning Inventory0—$0
Purchase 1300$6$1,800
Purchase 2400$8$3,200
Purchase 3600$9$5,400
Goods Available for Sale1,300$10,400

Ending Inventory Count: 200 units  |  Units Sold: 1,100

A. FIFO Ending Inventory

200 units (most recent purchase) × $9 = $1,800

B. LIFO Ending Inventory

200 units (oldest purchase) × $6 = $1,200

C. AVG Weighted Average Cost per Unit

$10,400 ÷ 1,300 = $8.00 per unit

D. AVG Average-Cost Ending Inventory

200 × $8.00 = $1,600
Think About It: FIFO does the best job of reporting ending inventory on the balance sheet at an amount closest to current market value, because FIFO assigns the most recent (most current) costs to ending inventory.
Demo 9-4: GAS, COGS & Ending Inventory – 3 Methods

Johnson Company (June):

ItemUnitsCost/UnitTotal
Jun 1 Beginning Inventory225$5$1,125
Jun 12 Purchase525$6$3,150
Jun 23 Purchase750$7$5,250
Goods Available for Sale1,500$9,525

Units Sold: 1,300  |  Ending Inventory: 200 units

A(1) FIFO

Ending Inventory: 200 units × $7 = $1,400
COGS: $9,525 − $1,400 = $8,125
Proof: 225 × $5 = $1,125 + 525 × $6 = $3,150 + 550 × $7 = $3,850 → $8,125

A(2) LIFO

Ending Inventory: 200 units × $5 = $1,000
COGS: $9,525 − $1,000 = $8,525
Proof: 750 × $7 = $5,250 + 525 × $6 = $3,150 + 25 × $5 = $125 → $8,525

A(3) AVG

Weighted Average Cost: $9,525 ÷ 1,500 = $6.35 per unit
Ending Inventory: 200 × $6.35 = $1,270
COGS: 1,300 × $6.35 = $8,255
Proof: $9,525 − $1,270 = $8,255

B. Comparison to FIFO & LIFO

Average-cost values fall BETWEEN FIFO and LIFO for both ending inventory and COGS. This is because weighted average blends all costs, whereas FIFO uses oldest (lowest) costs for COGS and LIFO uses newest (highest) costs for COGS in a period of rising prices.

Demo 9-5: GAS, EI & COGS – 3 Methods

Jeters Company Inc.:

ItemUnitsCost/UnitTotal
Beginning Inventory100$4$400
Jun 12 Purchase400$6$2,400
Jun 23 Purchase250$11$2,750
Goods Available for Sale750$5,550

Ending Inventory Count: 75 units  |  Units Sold: 675

Part A: Goods Available for Sale

Total units: 100 + 400 + 250 = 750
Total cost: $400 + $2,400 + $2,750 = $5,550

Part B(1) FIFO

Ending Inventory: 75 units × $11 = $825
COGS: $5,550 − $825 = $4,725
Proof: 100 × $4 = $400 + 400 × $6 = $2,400 + 175 × $11 = $1,925 → $4,725

Part B(2) LIFO

Ending Inventory: 75 units × $4 = $300
COGS: $5,550 − $300 = $5,250
Proof: 250 × $11 = $2,750 + 400 × $6 = $2,400 + 25 × $4 = $100 → $5,250

Part B(3) AVG

Weighted Average Cost: $5,550 ÷ 750 = $7.40 per unit
Ending Inventory: 75 × $7.40 = $555
COGS: 675 × $7.40 = $4,995
Proof: $5,550 − $555 = $4,995

Part C: Summary of Results

FIFOLIFOAvg Cost
Goods Available for Sale$5,550$5,550$5,550
COGS$4,725$5,250$4,995
Ending Inventory$825$300$555

C.1 – Best method for Ending Inventory on Balance Sheet?

FIFO does the best job. FIFO ending inventory of $825 (75 units × $11) reflects the most recent purchase price of $11, which is closest to current market replacement cost.

C.2 – Best method for COGS on Income Statement?

LIFO does the best job. LIFO COGS of $5,250 includes the most recently purchased (most current-cost) units sold, providing better matching of current costs against current revenues.

C.3 – Why is COGS highest with LIFO and lowest with FIFO?

Because unit costs were rising over time ($4 → $6 → $11). In a period of rising prices:
• LIFO assigns newest (highest) costs to COGS → Highest COGS
• FIFO assigns oldest (lowest) costs to COGS → Lowest COGS
• Average-cost blends all → Middle COGS

If costs had been declining, the relationship would reverse.
Demo 9-6: Inventory Analysis & Assessment

Fedor Company (switched to JIT early in 20X7):

Data20X620X7
Sales Revenue$3,120,000$3,713,000
Cost of Goods Sold$1,200,000$1,425,000
Beginning Inventory$170,000$210,000
Ending Inventory$210,000$90,000

Part A: Inventory Turnover & Days in Inventory

Inventory Turnover = COGS ÷ Average Inventory
Days in Inventory = 365 ÷ Inventory Turnover
Average Inventory = (Beginning Inventory + Ending Inventory) ÷ 2

20X6 Calculations

Average Inventory = ($170,000 + $210,000) ÷ 2 = $190,000
Inventory Turnover = $1,200,000 ÷ $190,000 = 6.32 times
Days in Inventory = 365 ÷ 6.32 = 57.75 days

20X7 Calculations

Average Inventory = ($210,000 + $90,000) ÷ 2 = $150,000
Inventory Turnover = $1,425,000 ÷ $150,000 = 9.50 times
Days in Inventory = 365 ÷ 9.50 = 38.42 days
Metric20X620X7
Inventory Turnover6.32 times9.50 times
Days in Inventory57.75 days38.42 days

Part B: Discussion

B.1 – Changes in Inventory, Turnover & Days – Consistent with JIT?

(a) Amount of Inventory Change:
Ending inventory decreased dramatically from $210,000 (20X6) to $90,000 (20X7) = $120,000 decrease (57.1% reduction). Average inventory also declined from $190,000 to $150,000.

(b) Inventory Turnover & Days in Inventory Changes:
• Inventory Turnover increased from 6.32 to 9.50 times (50.6% increase)
• Days in Inventory decreased from 57.75 to 38.42 days (33.5% improvement)

YES, these results are entirely consistent with Just-in-Time (JIT):
✓ Lower inventory levels – goods received only as needed (confirmed by $120,000 decrease)
✓ Higher inventory turnover – inventory moves faster (confirmed by 6.32 → 9.50)
✓ Fewer days in inventory – less time goods spend on hand (confirmed by 57.75 → 38.42)

JIT benefits: Reduced carrying costs, storage costs, obsolescence risk, and improved cash flow.

B.2 – What happened to sales revenue?

Sales Revenue increased from $3,120,000 (20X6) to $3,713,000 (20X7) = $593,000 increase (19.0%).

Possible Explanations:
1. Improved customer satisfaction from JIT → faster delivery, fresher products
2. Cost savings passed to customers → lower prices driving higher volume
3. Freed-up capital from reduced inventory → reinvested in marketing & R&D
4. General market growth or expansion into new markets
5. Better product availability due to JIT responsiveness
6. Improved profitability enables reinvestment in growth initiatives

📌 Key Takeaways for All Demos

Inventory Ownership (Demo 9-1)

  • Know FOB terms, consignment rules, and what qualifies as inventory
  • Audit physical counts against these ownership criteria

Inventory Methods (Demos 9-2 through 9-5)

  • FIFO: Best for balance sheet (current market value for Ending Inventory)
  • LIFO: Best for income statement (current market matching for COGS)
  • Average-Cost: Middle ground between both
  • In rising prices: FIFO EI highest, LIFO COGS highest
  • In declining prices: Relationship reverses

Inventory Analysis (Demo 9-6)

  • Higher turnover = faster selling = lower carrying costs
  • JIT reduces inventory while maintaining/increasing sales
  • Monitor trends year-over-year for operational efficiency
